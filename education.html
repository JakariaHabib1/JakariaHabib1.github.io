Capstone B.Sc. Thesis (ICE-4210) Department Honors Research

Physics-Based Musculoskeletal Simulation and Inverse Modeling for Latent Neuromuscular Delay Estimation

Advisor: Md. Imran Hossain (Assistant Professor, Department of Information & Communication Engineering, PUST)

Electromechanical delay (EMD: 15–60 ms) between neural excitation and muscular force generation critically governs human motor stability, yet experimental surface EMG suffers from 10–30 ms onset detection artifacts. This work establishes an end-to-end data-driven inverse modeling framework in Stanford OpenSim 4.5 using the 3DGaitModel2354 lower-limb architecture, inverting non-linear Thelen Hill-type activation ODEs across 45,445 labeled observations spanning \(\tau \in [0.015, 0.060\text{ s}]\).

Key Empirical Findings & Quantitative Benchmarks
  • Inverse Mapping Precision: Achieved \(R^2 > 0.998\) with Mean Absolute Error \(\text{MAE} < 0.10\text{ ms}\) (\(1 \times 10^{-4}\text{ s}\)), capturing fine-scale temporal delay accounting for less than 0.22% of total physiological variation.
  • Causal Information Hierarchy: Decomposed predictive contribution showing \(\sim 93.6\%\) of timing information resides in muscle activation dynamics versus only \(\sim 6.4\%\) in joint kinematics.
  • Primary Muscle Driver (SHAP): Identified Rectus Femoris as the primary functional pathway (60.5% feature importance); isolated ablation induced a 20-fold error surge (\(R^2\) falling from 0.998 to 0.777).
  • Proof of Continuity: Mathematically proved the learned mapping operates as a continuous operator rather than a discrete classifier, achieving errors 50–100\(\times\) lower than the fundamental discretization limit (\(\epsilon \ll \frac{1}{2}\Delta\tau_{\min}\)).
  • Perturbation Robustness: Validated under \(+7\%\) femoral mass anatomical perturbation, confirming bounded error (\(|\hat{\tau} - \tau| \ll 0.045\text{ s}\)) and structural stability against biomechanical distribution shifts.

Capstone B.Sc. Thesis (ICE-4210)

OpenSim Biomechanical Neuromuscular Latency Estimation

Developed a programmatic C-binding Python pipeline with the Stanford OpenSim engine to generate a 45,445-trial synthetic dataset. Modeled non-linear physiological latency with Random Forest regression, achieving an empirical prediction accuracy of R² > 0.98 alongside global SHAP feature importance interpretability.
